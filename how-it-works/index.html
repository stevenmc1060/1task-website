A shared hypothesis

Productivity isn't fundamentally a task management problem.

It's a context, prioritization, and execution problem.

The hypothesis

The problem isn't managing tasks.
It's managing everything around them.

Tasks are only part of the picture. Information is fragmented, commitments are distributed, and priorities shift. The mental overhead is deciding what matters, what needs a decision, and what to do next.

Capture → Understand → Prioritize → Act

Four connected ideas. Not a rigid process.

Each product applies the approach in its own environment, with different features and levels of maturity.

01

Capture

Bring thoughts, tasks, notes, ideas, and commitments together without excessive sorting.

Example“Ask the team about the beta rollout.”
02

Understand

Use available context to relate information to projects, goals, and existing commitments.

ExampleConnect the note with a launch project.
03

Prioritize

Consider what deserves attention, what needs a decision, and what can wait.

ExampleReview an open decision before Friday.
04

Act

Choose a meaningful next step. Use available assistance where the product supports it.

ExamplePrepare an update for your review.

Examples illustrate the shared philosophy; they do not imply identical features in both products.
